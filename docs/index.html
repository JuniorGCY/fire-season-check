Solid outline: the dates you picked. Dashed outline: provisional weeks (near-real-time data, may change). Empty squares: too few hotspots in the average to compare. “Normal” is the average of the previous 10 years for the same week.

By month

Same colours as the weekly calendar. A week belongs to the month of its Thursday. Months are compared per week with the median of the same month in the previous 10 years, so months with 4 and 5 weeks compare fairly. Dashed outline: partial month, or provisional (near-real-time data, may change).

The first two files list counts per week and per cell. The individual file lists every detection in your area and dates: date, time (UTC), latitude, longitude, sensor and fire radiative power (MW). Coordinates are rounded to about 100 m. The last column, harmonized_weight, is 1 for MODIS before 2012 and the monthly MODIS/VIIRS ratio for VIIRS afterwards, so its sum is the harmonized count shown above. Original files: NASA FIRMS. Hotspots are satellite detections, not confirmed fires.
